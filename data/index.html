Bank Customer Churn Prediction & Retention Recommender

End-to-end machine learning system that predicts bank customer churn, estimates expected financial loss, and recommends targeted retention actions. Built across three Jupyter notebooks, with separate training, evaluation, and production-style inference on a new batch of ~15k customers.

Problem

A retail bank wants to reduce customer churn. When customers close accounts or move their primary banking elsewhere, the bank loses deposits, loan balances, and cross-sell opportunities.

The goal of this project is to:

  • Predict which customers are most likely to churn, and
  • Prioritize retention efforts on customers where the expected financial loss is largest.

Because outreach capacity and incentives are limited, the system focuses on the top ~20% highest-risk customers while capturing almost all of the expected loss.

Project Flow

This project is organized as a three-step pipeline:

1. Data prep & feature engineering (01_bank_churn_eda_features.ipynb)

  • Load raw dataset (bank_customer_churn_copy.csv).
  • Drop leakage and identity columns: CustomerId, names, address, contact info, Churn Reason, Churn Date.
  • Engineer banking-specific features: tenure_bucket, high_balance, has_complaints, Occupation_freq.
  • One-hot encode low-cardinality categoricals (Gender, Marital Status, etc.).
  • Split into train / validation / test and save as Parquet files in processed_bank/.

2. Modeling & recommender (02_bank_churn_modeling_recommender.ipynb)

  • Load processed train.parquet, val.parquet, test.parquet.
  • Train Logistic Regression (baseline) and XGBoost (final model).
  • Evaluate with ROC-AUC, PR-AUC, Recall@K, and lift curves.
  • Compute churn scores, approximate CLV, and expected lost value.
  • Segment customers and assign rule-based retention actions.
  • Export full results to outputs_bank/bank_churn_recommendations.csv and save the model to models/xgb_bank_churn.json.

3. Inference on new customers (03_inference_on_new_customers.ipynb)

  • Load ~15.6k new customers from new_bank_customers.csv.
  • Apply the exact same preprocessing and feature engineering as training.
  • Align feature columns to the training schema and score with the saved XGBoost model.
  • Recompute CLV, expected lost value, segment, and recommended action.
  • Export production-style output to outputs_bank/new_customers_churn_predictions.csv.

Data & Features

The labeled dataset contains around 100k customers with demographics, relationship attributes, and financials. A separate file (~15.6k customers) is used for scoring only.

Inputs

  • Demographics: gender, marital status, education, dependents, income.
  • Relationship: customer tenure, segment (retail / SME), preferred channel, number of products.
  • Financials: balance, credit score, credit history length, outstanding loans.
  • Risk & satisfaction: number of complaints.

Engineered (Notebook 1)

  • tenure_bucket – 0–6m, 6–12m, 1–3y, 3y+.
  • high_balance – 1 if balance above train median.
  • has_complaints – 1 if any complaint.
  • Occupation_freq – frequency-encoded occupation; replaces a 600+ category string column.
Leakage handling: columns known only after churn (like Churn Reason and Churn Date) and personal identifiers are removed before modeling to keep the system realistic and deployable.

Modeling & Evaluation

Churn prediction is framed as a binary classification problem on an imbalanced dataset (~12% churn rate). To handle this, class weights are used and metrics focus on ranking quality.

Models

  • Logistic Regression: baseline, class-weighted, interpretable.
  • XGBoost: gradient-boosted trees, tuned for non-linear interactions and class imbalance.

Key evaluation metrics

  • ROC-AUC and PR-AUC on validation and test splits.
  • Recall@Top 5%, 10%, 20% highest-risk customers (business-relevant).
  • Lift curves vs random targeting.
In practice, the bank can only contact a small fraction of its customers each cycle. Evaluating Recall@K and lift answers the question: “If we only contact the top 10–20% most at-risk customers, how much churn do we capture?”

CLV & Recommendation Engine

To prioritize interventions by business value, each customer is assigned an approximate customer lifetime value (clv_est) and an expected lost value if they churn:

clv_est ≈ Balance + 0.5 × OutstandingLoans + 200 × NumOfProducts

expected_lost_value = churn_score × clv_est

Customers are then segmented using tenure, balance, products, and complaints into:

  • affluent_multi_product
  • high_complaint_risk
  • new_customer
  • single_product_low_engagement
  • mass_market

A rule-based recommender assigns targeted actions to customers in the top ~20% by churn score:

  • Affluent multi-product → personal banker outreach & rate offer.
  • High complaint risk → service recovery & possible fee refund.
  • New customers → onboarding support & welcome offer.
  • Single-product low engagement → cross-sell bundle offer.
  • Others above cutoff → general retention email.
  • Below cutoff → no_action to save budget.

System Pipeline

End-to-End Flow
  1. Ingest and clean historical labeled data (Notebook 1).
  2. Engineer features and encode categoricals; split into train/val/test; save to processed_bank/.
  3. Train and compare Logistic Regression and XGBoost (Notebook 2).
  4. Select XGBoost, compute churn scores, CLV, expected lost value.
  5. Segment customers and assign rule-based retention actions; export to bank_churn_recommendations.csv.
  6. For new customers, reload the model and reapply the same preprocessing (Notebook 3).
  7. Score new customers → churn_score, clv_est, expected_lost_value, segment, recommended_action.
  8. Export production-ready scoring file to new_customers_churn_predictions.csv.

Files Produced

  • processed_bank/train.parquet, val.parquet, test.parquet
  • outputs_bank/bank_churn_recommendations.csv
  • models/xgb_bank_churn.json
  • outputs_bank/new_customers_churn_predictions.csv
